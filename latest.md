## 1. Lead Story  
**Headline & Summary**  
*Scientists call for an international body to oversee advanced AI* – A coalition of leading AI researchers, Nobel laureates, and tech CEOs issued a joint statement urging the United Nations to create a standing oversight agency for “frontier‑level” artificial intelligence systems. The appeal follows a string of breakthroughs—including Google’s Gemini 4 Argon robotics model and AI‑driven proofs that have stunned mathematicians— that are compressing the timeline for transformative AI capabilities.  

**Long‑term Significance (1‑5 years)**  
- **Regulatory foothold:** An internationally recognised body could lock in safety standards before the next generation of self‑optimising models (e.g., Gemini 5, emergent AGI‑style systems) reaches deployment.  
- **Risk mitigation:** Centralised monitoring may curb race‑to‑deployment dynamics that currently push unsafe releases, reducing the probability of catastrophic alignment failures.  
- **Innovation pacing:** Formal oversight could shape funding flows, steering public‑private R&D toward transparent, auditable pathways and away from “black‑box” arms‑race projects.

The call follows months of high‑profile AI achievements: Google’s Gemini 4 Argon demonstrated autonomous manipulation of physical objects with sub‑centimetre precision; AI‑generated proofs (highlighted in NYT and WBUR pieces) have outperformed human mathematicians on longstanding conjectures. Simultaneously, dystopian narratives (The Atlantic) have amplified public anxiety. The oversight demand builds on prior voluntary coalitions (e.g., the 2024 Partnership on AI) but pushes for a legally binding, globally‑mandated entity—signalling a shift from self‑regulation to institutional governance.
