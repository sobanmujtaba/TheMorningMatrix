## 1. Lead Story  
**Headline & Summary**  
*United Airlines blocks AI agents from its booking platform over security concerns* – United announced it will actively block large‑language‑model (LLM)‑driven bots and “AI travel assistants” from accessing its reservation system, citing a surge in credential‑theft attempts, fraudulent itinerary changes, and data‑privacy violations. The airline will require all traffic to be human‑verified via CAPTCHAs and will share a blacklist of known AI endpoints with industry peers.  

**Long‑term Significance (1‑5 years)**  
- **Operational security**: The move sets a precedent for legacy carriers and travel aggregators to treat AI agents as a distinct threat vector, likely spurring a wave of “AI‑traffic filtering” solutions.  
- **Regulatory ripple**: U.S. DOT and the FAA have already hinted at guidance on “automated booking compliance.” United’s pre‑emptive stance could shape forthcoming rules, accelerating a compliance market for AI‑traffic monitoring.  
- **Consumer experience**: As travelers increasingly rely on generative‑AI assistants (e.g., chat‑based itinerary planners), a ban may create friction, prompting a push for “official” airline‑sanctioned AI APIs.
